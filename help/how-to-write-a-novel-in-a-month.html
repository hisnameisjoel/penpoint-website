Writing a Novel in a Month with Penpoint

Fifty thousand words in thirty days comes to 1,667 words a day. That is a real pace, but it is a steady one, and a little preparation makes it much easier to keep. This guide is a checklist for the whole month: what to set up beforehand, what to do each day, and how to be sure your draft is safe when you finish.

It fits Novel November, any 50,000-word challenge, or a month-long deadline you set for yourself.

The Short Version

  1. Preptober: set up your book, your cast, your places, and a loose chapter outline.
  2. Pick your daily number (1,667 words) and watch it in your writing stats.
  3. Draft in Manuscript Mode. Write first; worldbuild later.
  4. Save a snapshot each week, and let backups protect the rest.
  5. When you finish, export a Word file.

Preptober: Plan Before You Write

Preptober is the planning month before the writing starts. You do not need a finished plan. You need just enough that you never sit down to a blank page wondering what happens next.

  • Create your book: Creating Your First Book. Choose a starting template that fits your genre (Understanding Book Template Types); you can change everything later.
  • Meet your cast: Characters. Start with the people in your first few scenes and add the rest as they turn up.
  • Map your places: Locations. A few key places are plenty to begin with.
  • Gather your ideas: the Notebook holds research, the rules of your world, and loose thoughts in one place.
  • Outline your chapters and scenes: Creating Chapters and Scenes builds the structure. The Corkboard lays your chapters out as index cards you can rearrange, and Placeholder Cards hold a spot for chapters you have not figured out yet.

The outline carries straight into the writing: the chapters and scenes you plan are the ones you write in Manuscript Mode.

Tip: Plan in scenes, not just chapters. If a scene runs around 1,500 to 2,000 words, one scene a day carries you to the finish line.

Set a Word Count Goal

Pick your number before day one. For 50,000 words in 30 days it is 1,667 a day. These checkpoints keep you honest:

  • Day 7: about 11,700 words
  • Day 14: about 23,300 words
  • Day 21: about 35,000 words
  • Day 28: about 46,700 words
  • Day 30: 50,000 words

Penpoint keeps count for you. Click the bar chart icon in the toolbar at the top of the screen to open your writing stats. Under Today, Manuscript shows the words you have added to your manuscript so far today. That is the number to compare with 1,667. Worldbuilding words are counted separately, so planning notes never pad your total. Below it, Manuscript size shows how long your book is and how much it changed today.

In Manuscript Mode, the bar along the bottom of the writing area shows the word count of whatever you are viewing. Click it to add your Book's total and watch the number climb toward 50,000. See Manuscript Mode.

Your Writing Journey, in the same panel, counts your current streak and your longest streak of consecutive writing days. Week two is where many drafts stall, and a short day still keeps a streak going. On a bad day, write something small and stop.

To see how long you actually spend writing, start the writing timer from the clock at the top of the screen. See Tracking Your Time.

Your Daily Routine: Write First, Worldbuild Later

The month goes better when drafting and planning stay separate. Draft first. Fix the world afterward.

  • Draft in Manuscript Mode: click Manuscript, the pen icon at the top of the left sidebar, to switch to the writing side, where your planned chapters and scenes are waiting.
  • Keep your world beside you: Minipen is the sidebar that shows your characters, locations, and notes next to the page, so checking a name or a detail never means leaving your draft.
  • Do not stop to build: when someone turns up unexpectedly, press Ctrl+N (Cmd+N on Mac) to open Quick Create and jot them down, then keep writing. Fill in the details after the month ends. Quick Add Menu shows how it works.
  • Write in sprints: Flowstate runs timed cycles of writing and editing, and locks each finished sentence behind you so you keep moving forward instead of revising. The default is 15 minutes of writing and 5 of editing, and you can change both.
  • Go fullscreen: click Fullscreen Mode in the bottom-left corner of the writing area (or press F11 on Windows and Linux) to hide the toolbar and sidebars and leave just your page. Fullscreen Mode and Flowstate work independently, so use either, both, or neither.

Protect Your Draft

  • Autosave: Penpoint saves as you type. There is no save button to remember.
  • Automatic backups: Penpoint backs up your books on its own and lets you restore an earlier copy. Backup & Restore covers how to keep a second copy in a Dropbox, OneDrive, or iCloud folder.
  • Weekly snapshots: at the end of each week, click the Manuscript versions button in the Manuscript toolbar and save a snapshot with a note like “Week 1, 11,700 words.” A snapshot is a read-only copy of your whole manuscript that you can open and copy text from, and you can keep up to 10 per book. See Manuscript Snapshots.
  • Before a big rewrite: Scene Drafts save alternate takes of a scene without losing the original.

Penpoint Cloud for Novel November

Penpoint Cloud keeps an encrypted copy of your books online and, with the Sync plan, keeps the same book up to date across your computers. It is optional, and your books always live on your own computer first.

During Novel November 2026, Penpoint offers a free month of Cloud:

  • What you get: the full Penpoint Sync plan, with no card needed.
  • Who can claim it: accounts that do not already have a subscription.
  • When it ends: December 1, 2026 at 12:00 UTC, whenever you start.
  • After that: Cloud pauses on your devices. Subscribe to keep it going; otherwise your cloud copy is kept for 30 days and then deleted. The books on your computer always stay yours.

To claim it, open Settings > Penpoint Cloud and choose Set up Penpoint Cloud. When you are eligible, the plan step shows a Free through November card with a Start now button instead of the usual plans. See Setting Up Penpoint Cloud for the full walkthrough and Managing Your Subscription for plans and canceling.

Note: Cloud is separate from the free trial download. The trial is Penpoint itself; Cloud is an optional online service with its own account. If you do not see Penpoint Cloud in your Settings, your copy does not include it, and everything else in this guide works without it.

Tip: If you set up Cloud, save your Recovery Kit. It is the spare key to your encrypted books. See Your Password and Recovery Kit.

Finish and Export

When you reach your number, or the month runs out, export the draft. In Manuscript Mode, click the Export manuscript button in the toolbar at the top of the writing area and choose a format. Word (.docx) is the safe pick because it opens almost everywhere; EPUB, PDF, Markdown, plain text, and RTF are there too. Exporting never changes your manuscript. See Exporting Manuscripts.

A finished draft is a first draft. Step away for a while; your snapshots and drafts will be waiting when you come back to revise.
