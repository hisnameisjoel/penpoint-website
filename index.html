Frequently Asked Questions

What is a character bible?

It's basically a reference sheet for every character in your story — names, traits, relationships, backstories, eye color, all of it. The goal is consistency. When you're 300 pages deep and can't remember if your protagonist's mentor had a limp or a scar, the character bible knows.

What is a story bible?

Same idea as a character bible, but for your whole world. Locations, timelines, plot threads, worldbuilding — everything. Some people call them series bibles or show bibles. They're especially useful when you're writing book 3 and need to remember what happened in book 1.

What is Penpoint?

A desktop app for fiction writers who need to keep track of characters, locations, relationships, and all the little details that pile up across a book or series. Character bible + story bible + search, in one place.

Who is Penpoint for?

Writers, mostly. Fiction authors are the core audience, but people also use it for nonfiction, historical research, and tabletop RPG campaigns. Whether you've got five characters or five hundred, it's just a good place to keep everything about your project in one spot.

What makes Penpoint different from Notion or Scrivener?

Notion is a general-purpose wiki — great for a lot of things, but not built for fiction. Scrivener is a drafting tool. Penpoint is neither. It's specifically for tracking your story: relationship maps, visual filtering, cross-referenced characters and locations. One job, done well.

Does Penpoint work offline?

Completely. Everything lives on your computer. No internet, no account, no cloud. Your story details don't leave your machine unless you export them yourself.

Can I manage multiple books or a series?

Yes — there's a multi-book library built in. Transfer characters and locations between books, filter by installment, or just view everything across the whole series at once.

What is Flowstate?

A writing mode where sentences lock behind you as you type. You literally can't go back and rewrite the same paragraph for the tenth time. Timed cycles alternate between writing sprints and editing windows. It's brutal, but it works.

How much does Penpoint cost?

$40, once. No subscription. We're currently in beta — join the waitlist to get early access.

What platforms does Penpoint support?

Windows, macOS (Intel and Apple Silicon), and Linux. It's a native desktop app — not a web app running in a browser.

Can I import my existing character notes?

There's an import wizard, but fair warning — it currently reads Penpoint's own export format, not files from other apps. So you can export from one Penpoint project and import into another, but you can't drag in a Scrivener file and have it just work. For notes from other tools, copy-paste is still the move for now. If enough people want import support for a specific app, we're open to building it.
